Practical Logistics

Bolt vs. Train/Bus — Every Leg (2 people)

Cash — How Much & When to Withdraw

Short answer: Bring €200–250 in cash total for the trip. Portugal is highly card-friendly — the only places you'll reliably need cash are a handful of small tascas, the Bifanas de Afonso, coin-operated bag lockers at Faro station, and the occasional market vendor or ginjinha stand.

Where that number comes from — tallying every known cash-only item on this itinerary: Bifanas de Afonso ~€10–16 · Faro lockers ~€5–6 · ginjinha (both bars) ~€6–8 · Nazaré bag-holding tip ~€5–10 · Tavira ferry round trip ~€8 · market stalls/small tascas buffer ~€40–60. That's roughly €75–110 in identified needs — the €200 pull below leaves comfortable headroom for anything cash-only you run into that isn't on the list.

Where Card Works Fine
  • All train/bus ticket machines — contactless or card
  • Virtually all restaurants and cafés
  • Supermarkets (Minipreço, Pingo Doce, Lidl, Aldi)
  • Bolt/Uber — in-app, no cash
  • All accommodation — paid via Airbnb
  • Livraria Lello, Torre de Belém, all major attractions
  • Most market stalls (increasingly card)
Where You'll Need Cash
  • Bifanas de Afonso (D4) — cash only, stated on listing
  • Faro station bag lockers (D8) — coins, ~€5–6
  • Ginjinha bars (D4 evening) — €1.50/shot, cash preferred
  • Nazaré restaurant holding bags for the day — tip in cash
  • Market produce/fish stalls — some cash-only vendors
  • Tavira Island ferry — cash likely (~€2/pp)
  • Small tascas — occasional cash-only spots
ATM Strategy
  • Use Multibanco ATMs (Portugal's national network — the blue/orange machines everywhere). Avoid standalone "Euronet" ATMs in tourist areas — they charge dynamic currency conversion fees.
  • Withdraw in Porto on Day 1 or 2 — take out €150–200 once. Multibanco at any Portuguese bank (CGD, BPI, Millennium BCP, Santander). Look for the "MB" logo.
  • Top up before Tavira if needed — smaller Algarve towns have fewer ATMs. €50 buffer is enough for Tavira + Lagos.
  • Card to use for purchases: Ideally a no-foreign-transaction-fee card (Charles Schwab, Capital One credit card, Wise). Standard Visa/Mastercard work everywhere — just decline dynamic currency conversion if asked ("charge in EUR, not USD"). See the ATM warning below before using a Capital One debit card for cash.
  • Don't exchange at the airport — rates are poor. Skip IAD exchange entirely; Multibanco in Porto on arrival is the right move.
⚠️ Check which network your Capital One card is on before you rely on it at an ATM. Capital One moved its debit/ATM cards from Mastercard to Discover starting mid-2025. Discover has real acceptance gaps at European ATMs — travelers have reported entire countries where no ATM would take the new card. If your card shows the Discover logo (not Visa or Mastercard), don't plan on it for Multibanco withdrawals — bring a Visa- or Mastercard-network debit card as the primary cash source, and keep the Capital One card only as a backup to test, not the plan. (A Capital One credit card for purchases is unaffected — this is specifically a debit/ATM card + cash-withdrawal issue.)
Summary: Withdraw €200 from a Multibanco ATM in Porto on Day 1 or 2, using a Visa/Mastercard-network debit card. Use card for everything you can. Keep €50 in reserve going into the Algarve. You should finish the trip with minimal leftover cash.

Weather & Packing

Late September/early October in Portugal is generally mild and dry, but Porto and the north see more rain than the Algarve — and several days here are outdoor-heavy with no rain fallback built in (Douro boat tour D1, Guincho D7, Boca do Inferno D6, Ponta da Piedade D10). Pack a compact umbrella and a light rain jacket for both of you — cheap insurance against losing one of the paid, non-refundable outdoor bookings to weather.
